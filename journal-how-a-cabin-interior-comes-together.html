How a Cabin Interior Comes Together

26 October 2026 · 1 min read

Walls, ceiling, floor and doors are chosen separately, so a cabin can echo the house around it. A short guide to the materials and how they combine.

A home elevator is a small room you step into several times a day. That is why every SINNVO cabin is finished like a room: in stone, brushed metal, walnut and mirror, rather than as a box hidden inside a wall.

Start from a collection

There are thirteen cabin collections, each with its own mood. A few examples of how differently they read:

  • Muyu sets dark walnut panels against 304 black-titanium mirror, with concealed lighting.
  • Tangyue combines Pandora stone with a milk-white panel and rose steel trim.
  • Jingjie is built around a sapphire art-stone panel with mirror stainless steel.

The designs draw on Eastern and Western heritage, from porcelain, the Baoxiang flower and Ru-kiln celadon to the lines of the Parthenon.

Then adapt it to your home

Every collection can be adapted. Hall doors, ceilings and flooring are specified separately, so the elevator can echo the materials already used in your home.

  • Hall doors come in five finishes: brushed steel, black titanium, titanium gold, rose gold and etched bronze.
  • Ceilings can be starlit, meteor or glazed and light-transmitting.
  • Flooring is sintered stone or PVC as standard, with inlaid marble as an option.

The materials behind the finish

Sintered stone, brushed and mirror steel, walnut panel, laminated safety glass and industrial-grade aluminium all pass through the same workshop that engineers the shaft structure, so materials and structure are designed together rather than bolted on afterwards.

Cabin decoration and non-standard manufacturing is where SINNVO started, and it is still the core skill behind every finish. Every panel is hand-finished and quality-checked before it leaves the workshop.
