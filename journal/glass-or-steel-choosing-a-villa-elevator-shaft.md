People spend weeks choosing the cabin. The shaft gets decided in a minute, yet it is the part of the elevator the whole household walks past every day. SINNVO builds shaft frames in two materials, and they suit different rooms.

## Steel: the frame disappears

A steel structure is the cost-effective option. It is protected by an anti-rust coating, and its outer cladding is finished later by your own contractor. That means it can take on any interior: plaster to match the walls, wood panelling, stone.

Choose steel when the elevator should be hidden, when it sits inside a wall line, or when the shaft runs through rooms with different finishes on each floor.

## Aluminium and glass: the frame becomes a feature

An aluminium alloy frame forms an independent glazed shaft. It needs no extra footprint, lets light pass through, and uses industrial-grade aluminium with laminated safety glass for a longer service life.

Choose glass when the elevator stands in a double-height hall, beside a stair or next to a window, where a closed box would block the light and the view.

## Frame colours

The frame comes in six metal colours: silver white, radiant silver, champagne gold, rose gold, sky blue and cinnabar red.

Silver white and radiant silver sit quietly in most modern interiors. Champagne gold and rose gold pick up brass or warm metal fittings elsewhere in the house. Sky blue and cinnabar red make the shaft the focal point of the room.

## Glass tints

The glass comes in six tints: clear, ultra-clear, Euro grey, star blue-grey, gold tea and Euro tea.

Clear and ultra-clear keep the cabin fully visible. Ultra-clear removes the faint green edge of ordinary glass, which shows most on tall shafts. The grey and blue-grey tints soften what you see of the rails and running gear. The tea tints add warmth and pair well with champagne gold frames and walnut cabins.

## Match the shaft to the cabin

The shaft and the cabin are chosen separately, but they are seen together. A glass shaft shows the cabin from every floor, so its interior becomes part of the room.
